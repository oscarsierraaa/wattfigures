Electricity in the United Kingdom costs £0.32 per kilowatt-hour — $0.43 once converted — which puts it +43 % above the median of the 23 countries tracked on this site. That is the price with all taxes included, for a household in the 2,500–4,999 kWh a year band — one definition applied to every country, because mixing tax-inclusive and tax-exclusive figures is what puts most country comparisons out by 15% or more.

What that means in practice: a household using 3,700 kWh a year pays about £1,184 for the electricity itself. The number on your own bill will differ — that is what the retailer table below is for.

How clean the electricity is

Price is only half of what a kilowatt-hour costs. The grid in the United Kingdom emits 185 grams of CO₂-equivalent per kWh on a lifecycle basis, averaged over a year of generation. For the same 3,700 kWh household that is about 685 kg of CO₂ a year.

For scale: the cleanest grid tracked here is Norway at 20 g/kWh, the dirtiest is South Africa at 950 g/kWh. The United Kingdom sits in the cleaner third, 8th cleanest of 23.

This matters for a practical reason as well as an environmental one: on a clean grid, swapping a gas boiler for a heat pump cuts emissions immediately. On a coal-heavy one, the same swap mostly moves the emissions from your street to the power station.

Standing charges are not in the per-kWh figure. For anything that matters, use the number on your own bill — every calculator here takes it.
